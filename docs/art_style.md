# Pixelica art style: right-angled miniatures

Goal: a delicate, orthogonal "miniature room" look at a low internal render
resolution, not big Minecraft-like blocks. Voxel resolution (how a prop is
authored) and render resolution (how many pixels the screen buffer has) are
independent: the same model must read well at Pixel Room TINY (160x120) and
STANDARD (256x192). These are game profiles, not `PIXELICA_TINY`/`PIXELICA_STANDARD`
engine constants; resolution is configured through `PIXELICA_Config`. Authoring density says nothing about runtime
performance; runtime cost follows the *meshed* triangle count.

## Authoring grid

* Props: 32-64 voxels along the longest side. Key furniture may go up to 128
  (the importer limit), but check triangle counts.
* One voxel is `1/16` world unit with the default import scale. Roughly
  1 world unit is 30 cm, so the desk (64 voxels = 4 units) is about 1.2 m.
* All models are authored in VOX axes: X right, Y depth, Z up, **front = -Y**.
  The importer turns that into engine X right, Y up, front = +Z (see
  `docs/asset_format.md`).
* Pivot `floor` (default): the model stands on Y = 0 and is centered
  horizontally on its declared `SIZE`. Keep `SIZE` tight around the shape so
  the pivot is where you expect.
* Do not author detail you cannot see at the target render resolution.
  Whether a 1-voxel feature survives depends on camera distance and render
  resolution; verify it with the TINY preview (see "Checking readability").

## Shape language

* Right angles dominate, but break silhouettes with steps: lips, grooves,
  handles, thin borders, inset panels, a 1-voxel overhang.
* Give every prop at least two materials/tones (frame vs. panel, ceramic vs.
  liquid, pot vs. leaves) and one small contrasting accent (metal handle,
  gold title stripe, flower).
* Keep a 1-voxel groove (a recessed row) wherever two parts meet, e.g. drawer
  fronts: the groove is a color/shape change that stays visible when the
  image is downscaled.
* Everything must be face-connected (no floating voxels). Diagonal-only
  contact does not count; the generator tests check connectivity.
* Thin walls are 1-2 voxels. Avoid interior cavities that can never be seen:
  they cost triangles for nothing (the importer keeps cavity faces).

## Color

* Use a custom palette per model (an `RGBA` chunk) and keep all colors opaque.
  Colors are quantized to RGB565 (truncating), so tones that differ by fewer
  than 8 in red/blue or 4 in green can collapse to one color on screen;
  separate tones by at least ~16 per channel when they must stay distinct.
* Palette index, not final color, controls greedy merging. Two palette
  entries are never merged even when they quantize to the same RGB565 value,
  so reuse one palette entry for all voxels meant to be the same material.
* Warm wood/terracotta against cool accents (blue stripe, green leaves) keeps
  props readable against the room's neutral floor texture.
* Shading is baked by hand: use a lighter top tone and a darker bottom/side
  tone (the engine renders unlit).

## Mesh budget

Triangle count after greedy meshing depends on how *flat* the model is, not on
voxel count. Long uniform surfaces merge into single quads; curved shapes
(discs, spheres) and one-voxel noise do not. Rules of thumb:

* Prefer straight runs of the same color over speckle or dithering.
* Spheres and discs are the most expensive shapes; keep radius small.
* Check the numbers with `python tools/pixelica_pack/pixelica_pack.py inspect model.p3mesh`
  (`triangle_count`, `vertex_count`). Hard importer limits are 65 535 vertices
  and 196 608 indices (65 536 triangles) per mesh; a single room prop should
  stay far below.

The floor texture `checker` is a 32 x 32 RGB565 checker (4 x 4 tiles of 8
pixels, 1-pixel grout line) designed to be sampled nearest. Pixel Room repeats
floor geometry and maps the full [0,1] UV range onto each tile; the sampler
clamps UVs and does not wrap them.

## Checking readability

1. Run `pixel_room_tiny` and `pixel_room` and compare the saved
   frames.
2. If a detail disappears at TINY, enlarge it to at least 2 voxels or give it
   a clearly different tone.
3. Re-run `python tools/pixelica_pack/generate_demo_assets.py --check` to confirm
   included compiled assets still match their sources.
